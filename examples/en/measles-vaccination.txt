Measles cases have risen sharply across Europe, prompting health officials to call for higher vaccination rates. The World Health Organization reported more than 42,000 measles cases in the region last year, compared with fewer than 1,000 the year before.

Dr. Hans Kluge, the WHO regional director, said the rise was driven by gaps in childhood immunisation during the pandemic. Many children missed their first dose of the MMR vaccine, which protects against measles, mumps and rubella.

Measles is one of the most contagious diseases known. A single infected person can spread the virus to as many as 18 others. Public health experts say at least 95% of children need two doses of the MMR vaccine to prevent outbreaks.

In London, the UK Health Security Agency has opened pop-up vaccination clinics in schools and shopping centres. Parents can book appointments online or walk in without a referral. Officials hope the clinics will reach families who struggle to see a general practitioner.

Vaccine hesitancy remains a challenge. Surveys show that misinformation on social media has made some parents doubt the safety of the MMR vaccine, despite decades of evidence. Health officials say trusted local voices, such as nurses and teachers, are the most effective messengers.
