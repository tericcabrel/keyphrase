Offshore wind farms are becoming the backbone of Britain's energy strategy. The government wants offshore wind capacity to reach 50 gigawatts by 2030, enough to power every home in the country.

The largest project, Dogger Bank Wind Farm, sits more than 130 kilometres off the Yorkshire coast. When complete, Dogger Bank will use 277 turbines, each taller than the Eiffel Tower. Engineers say the turbines can generate electricity even in moderate winds.

Grid connections remain the biggest obstacle. National Grid has warned that some offshore wind farms may wait years before they can connect to the transmission network. Developers also face rising steel prices and higher interest rates, which have pushed up the cost of building turbines at sea.

Supporters argue that offshore wind lowers household energy bills over time. A report from the Climate Change Committee found that every gigawatt of new offshore wind capacity reduces the need for gas imports. Critics, however, point to the impact on fishing grounds and seabirds.

Local communities along the Yorkshire coast have mixed views. Some welcome the jobs at the port of Hull, where turbine blades are manufactured. Others worry about new transmission lines crossing farmland.
